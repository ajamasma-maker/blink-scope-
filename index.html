What could explain a change?

  • A moving object, such as an asteroid
  • A variable or transient source
  • Image alignment or instrumental effects

Why compare observations?

A single image is a snapshot. Repeated observations add a time dimension, helping researchers investigate changing brightness or apparent position.

How this relates to SPHEREx

SPHEREx is an all-sky near-infrared spectral survey with 102 spectral bands. Those bands represent wavelengths; they are not 102 dates. A real implementation must compare appropriately calibrated observations of the same sky region.

Next scientific steps

Connect real SPHEREx observations from NASA/IPAC IRSA; align and calibrate image pairs; account for observation metadata and artifacts; cross-match candidate sources with established catalogs; then present candidate classifications with uncertainty. A visual difference alone is not evidence of a new planet.
